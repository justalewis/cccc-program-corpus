How to read these numbers

What is counted

Every count here is a count of names printed in a convention program. A program is a plan. It lists what CCCC accepted and scheduled, so it says who was invited onto the stage, and it does not say who attended, who showed up to speak, or who stayed to listen. The 2020 program describes a convention that was cancelled.

The charts shade these years. A change across a break may come from the source. For example, the step between 2023 and 2024 may come from the move from PDF programs to eShow, and the jump at 2027 comes from a listing that is still filling in.

Where the programs come from

1998 comes from the convention's web program in the Wayback Machine. 1999 comes from the PDFs that survive there, for ten of fourteen letter sections. 2004–2023 come from NCTE's program PDFs, and 2024–2027 from NCTE's eShow listings. 1994–1997 survive online only as front pages, and 2000–2003 are not online at all. The years before 1998 need the printed programs.

The PDF parser was scored against eShow for 2024–2026, the years both exist: it finds the listed names with precision 0.94–0.97 and recall 0.92–0.95. It has not been scored for earlier years, and a human check of 40 sessions is under way.

Names

People are joined across years when the printed names match exactly (case, punctuation and accents are folded), plus pairs a reviewer has marked as the same person. “Jim Brown” and “James Brown” are two names until a reviewer joins them, so near-duplicate names can inflate the count of first listings and lower the count of returners. Two different people with the same name become one, and names a reviewer has not checked are marked on their pages.

Institutions

A printed affiliation is matched to a record in ROR and, for US colleges, to IPEDS. IPEDS says whether a college is two-year (IPEDS level 2, or Carnegie basic class 1–14). Shares of institution types use people whose institution matched a US college in IPEDS. About 3% of printed affiliations match nothing: independent scholars, schools, publishers and a long tail.

Proposal areas

From 2004 through 2026 (except 2018) the programs print an area above most sessions. We read those lines as CCCC printed them. They agree with eShow's own track tags for every 2025 and 2026 session that has both. Workshops, special interest group meetings and many special sessions carry none, so shares are of sessions that have one. The families that join a renamed area across years (for example, Practices of Teaching Writing in 2005 and Approaches to Teaching and Learning in 2023) are a draft of ours, listed in data/area_crosswalk.csv, and they need a second reader. The lists of areas also differ by era (first-year composition and social justice become areas only later, and institutional-and-professional ends in 2019), so a change in a family's share can reflect CCCC's headings and not the field's interests.

Terms

The terms tool counts a word wherever it appears in a title, with word boundaries (“labor” does not match “collaboration”). A match shows that the word was in a title, not what the session argued. Open the titles before reading a trend.
